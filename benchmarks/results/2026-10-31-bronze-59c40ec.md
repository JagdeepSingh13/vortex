## Notes on these results

- **At 1,000 orders/s (~460 trades/s) the trigger sets the freshness.** Batches take 3–4 s (median)
  and the job idles between triggers, so lag is spread evenly over one interval: p50 ≈ 5 s,
  p99 ≈ 9.9 s with a 10 s trigger. A row becomes readable when its batch commits, about 3–4 s later.
- **At 3,000 orders/s (~1,430 trades/s) the job is saturated.** Median batch time is 10–16 s,
  longer than either trigger, so batches run back to back and the trigger stops mattering.
  A 2 s trigger was not fresher than a 10 s one; it was worse (lag p50 7.9–11.2 s vs 5.0–6.7 s),
  because the job was busy anyway and ended up with fewer, larger, slower batches.
- **So bronze's capacity on this laptop is roughly 1,500–2,000 trades/s** (Spark local mode with
  2 cores and a 1 GB driver, sharing the machine with Kafka, the engine and the simulator).
  The engine produces trades far faster than that (M5), so bronze is the slowest stage of the
  pipeline at high load. Raising it (more cores, larger batches, tuning the writer) is follow-up
  performance work, not done here.
- The 2 s run 2 shows 85,668 rows instead of 85,672: it was measured before bronze had caught up
  (batches up to 30 s). A later reconciliation of the whole topic found every trade exactly once
  (840,546 rows, 0 duplicates, 0 gaps).
- During this benchmark Windows briefly ran out of resources (a shell fork failed with
  0xC000009A): the laptop is near its limit with Kafka, Spark, the engine and the simulator.
